## The five essentials

Put whichever you know in your first message:

**Where** · **When** (exact dates) · **Who** (how many, any kids) · **Budget** (total) · **From** (defaults to your home city)

> Plan 5 days in Bali from Bengaluru, 12–16 December, for two of us. Relaxed pace, budget around ₹1.5 lakh total.

## Phrases that do things

| Say | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **“Save it”** | Creates the trip and copies the plan into its itinerary |
| **“Plan the whole trip”** | Flights, stays, things to do and the day-by-day plan |
| **“Lighten the rest of today”** | Moves or skips heavier stops; nothing changes until you tap |
| **“What's on today?”** | Today's plan for the trip you're on |
| **“Tuk tuk 800 LKR”** | Logs an expense and shows today's balance |
| **“Send my itinerary to Mum”** | A ready-to-send message with the plan and a link |
| **“Keep Mum posted on my flights”** | Flight updates for family (Elite) |
| **“Save this as a routine”** | Turns what you just did into a phrase you can reuse |
| **“How does this work?”** | Your own next step, and a replay of the tour |

## Habits that help

1. **Ask for the action**, not advice: "Add Tanah Lot to day 3", not "what about Tanah Lot?"
2. **Correct in small steps**: "cheaper hotels", "make day 3 quieter".
3. **State hard limits as limits**: "under ₹8,000 a night", "free cancellation".
4. **Name the trip** if you have more than one.
5. **Send, don't type**: links, photos, receipts, PDFs, voice notes and your location.
6. **Ask for the format**: a table, a checklist, an Excel file, a calendar file.
