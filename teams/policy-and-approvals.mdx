Mazer checks every trip against the traveller's grade before anyone sees it, and says what's inside the policy and what isn't. Travel admins set the policy on **Policy**, in four tabs: **Grades**, **Approvals**, **Payments and expenses** and **Your rules**. Everyone else reads it on **Your policy**, or under **Your policy** on **Book**, or asks Mazer:

<Prompt title="Your policy" text="What's my hotel cap in [Pune]?" why="Mazer answers from your company's policy for your grade, in your company's currency." />

Changes apply from the next request.

## Grades

A grade is the policy for a group of people, such as *Staff* and *Leadership*. On **Grades**, the list on the left shows each grade and how many people are in it. A new company starts with one grade, **Everyone**. **Add a grade** copies the one you're looking at; give it a **Grade name** and save it. While a grade has unsaved changes, a bar at the foot of the page offers to save them. Each person's grade is set on **People**.

### Flights

- **Cabin:** Economy, Premium economy or Business.
- **A better cabin on long flights:** Premium economy or Business when one flight of the trip is at least the hours you set. Mazer checks the cabin of every flight against it.
- **Fare above the cheapest:** a percentage, or an amount, whichever is more. The cheapest is the cheapest fare leaving within the hours you set in **Cheapest counted within**, either side of the chosen flight.
- **Book at least** so many days ahead.
- **One checked bag included.**

### Hotels, per night

A cap for each group of cities, and **Stars, at most**:

| | In India | Outside India |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top cap | **Metros** | **Major cities** |
| Second | **Tier 2 cities** | **Other cities** |
| Third | **Other cities** | |
| Outside your country | **Abroad** | **Abroad** |

- **In India**, cities follow the government's X, Y and Z classification: Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Pune and Ahmedabad are metros; cities like Jaipur, Kochi and Lucknow are tier 2; other places are tier 3.
- **Outside India**, your country's major cities get the top cap, such as Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah in the United Arab Emirates, or London, Manchester, Edinburgh and Birmingham in the United Kingdom. The rest of your country gets the second.
- **Abroad** is anywhere outside your company's country.
- **Your own cities and countries:** a cap for one place, such as *Mumbai* or *Japan*, used there instead of its group's cap.

### Daily allowance

- **Meals and local travel, in** your company's country, in your currency.
- **Abroad, in US dollars**, converted into your currency when a claim is checked.

It covers each day's meals, local travel and small costs, not hotels, flights, visas or client entertainment. Your company either checks claims against it or pays it as a per diem. See [The daily allowance](/teams/expenses-and-claims#the-daily-allowance).

### When a trip is outside the policy

Each grade chooses what happens to a trip outside its policy:

- **Send it for approval:** the approver sees what's outside and why. New grades start here.
- **Book it, with a reason:** no sign-off just for being outside; the traveller's reason is kept with the trip.
- **Don't allow it:** it can't be sent, and Mazer points to the options inside the policy.

## Your rules

Most companies already have a written travel policy. On **Your rules**, a travel admin adds it: **Upload** a PDF or text file, or **Paste text**. Mazer reads it and shows what it found, for you to check before anything changes:

- **Settings** it can check exactly, like the cabin, hotel caps, a cap for a named city and the daily allowance, go into the grade you choose. Amounts in another currency are left out.
- **Everything else** is kept as a rule, close to your document's own words, such as *No overnight flights before a client meeting.*

You can also write a rule yourself. For each rule, choose who it's for (everyone, or one grade) and whether Mazer checks it. When a trip may break a rule Mazer checks, or the rule asks for more on a trip like it, the approver sees the rule next to the trip, and the trip waits for their yes. Travellers see your rules on **Your policy**, and Mazer knows them when it answers questions about the policy.

## Who approves

On **Approvals**, under **Who approves**, choose when a person has to say yes:

- **Every trip:** the manager sees each one. New companies start here.
- **Only trips outside the policy:** trips inside it are approved at once. Trips abroad still go to the manager.
- **Nothing:** trips inside the policy are approved at once.

A trip outside the policy follows the grade's choice under [When a trip is outside the policy](#when-a-trip-is-outside-the-policy), and a trip that one of [your rules](#your-rules) applies to goes to the approver whichever you choose.

Each person's manager approves their trips. People without a manager go to the **Travel admin** you choose here, and nobody approves their own trip. A travel admin can also decide any trip that's waiting on someone else.

If the manager hasn't answered, they're reminded after the hours in **Remind the manager after** (4 to start), and the trip goes to the travel admin after **Then send it to the travel admin after** (24 to start). Choose **Save timings** after changing them.

### After the manager

**After the manager** adds steps for bigger trips, trips abroad and project spend:

- **Trips over** an amount also go to finance, the department head, or someone you name.
- **Trips abroad also go to** someone you name.
- **Project spend goes to the project's owner:** when a trip names a project, its owner approves too.
- **Over a budget goes to its owner:** when a trip takes a department's, cost centre's or project's budget over, its owner approves too. This is on to start.

A trip goes through the steps in that order, and the approver sees which step it's on, such as *Step 2 of 3*. The traveller hears as each step approves. Nobody is asked twice, and nobody approves their own trip.

### When an approver is away

In **Your details → When you're away**, an approver sets the dates (**From** and **Back after**) and **Who decides meanwhile**. Their approval steps go to that person until they're back, including any already waiting. **I'm back** ends it early. A travel admin can set it for someone else by asking Mazer on the desk: *"Set Daniel away next week with Mei deciding."* See [Ask Mazer on the desk](/teams/slack-and-mazer#ask-mazer-on-the-desk).

## Departments, cost centres and projects

In **Settings → Structure**, travel admins describe how the company is organised. Trips and expenses are filed under it, and approvals follow its owners.

- **Departments:** who people work for. The owner is the department head.
- **Cost centres:** where the spend is charged. Each person has one, and a trip can name another.
- **Projects:** the clients or projects a trip is for. The owner approves project spend when that rule is on.

Each can have a **Code**, an **Owner** and a **Budget** a month, a quarter or a year. Approved and booked trips count against a budget. The budget year follows your company's country: April to March in India, Japan, the UK and New Zealand, July to June in Australia, and January to December everywhere else. Quarters follow the same year. The approver sees how much of the budget is used, and how much with this trip. **Archive** hides one you no longer use.

### On every trip

In **Settings → Trip fields and codes**, under **On every trip**, make **Purpose**, **Cost centre** or **Project** required, and keep your list of **Purposes**, like *Client meeting*. Mazer asks for anything missing in chat before the trip goes to the approver, and **Book** asks for it in the review. A traveller's own cost centre is used unless they name another.

### Offices and legal entities

- **Offices:** where people are based, with a city and an address. Each person can have one, and **Book** finds hotels near an office.
- **Legal entities:** the companies your trips are billed to, with a legal name and a registered address, and one marked **Default**. A trip is billed to the traveller's office's entity, else the default. In India, each entity has its GSTIN and state, and a trip is billed to the entity in its state first.

### GL codes

**GL codes**, in **Settings → Trip fields and codes**, sets the account each kind of spend goes to: **Flights**, **Hotels**, **Meals**, **Local travel** and **Other expenses**. Zoho Books matches the account code, and the month's file for your accountant lists it on every line. In India, TallyPrime uses it as the ledger name. See [Reports and books](/teams/reports-and-books).

## More on Policy

On **Approvals**, under **Notices**:

- **Monday summary:** each Monday morning, the travel admins get what's waiting on them, who's away that week and what's been booked this month. On to start.
- **SOS reaches the travel admins:** when someone on a work trip sends an SOS from TripMazer, the travel admins hear too, as well as the traveller's emergency contact. Off to start; tell your team before you turn it on.

On **Payments and expenses**:

- **Savings rewards:** the traveller's share of what a trip comes in under the policy, from 0 (off) to 50%. When a trip inside the policy is booked for less than the policy allowed, the traveller earns that share of the saving. Trips booked for guests earn none. You pay it through payroll; TripMazer keeps count on **Reports**.
- **Paying for bookings:** the travel admin with the company card, or the traveller with their own. See [Who books and pays](/teams/booking-trips#who-books-and-pays).
- **Expenses on trips:** a daily allowance (per diem) and mileage rates. See [Expenses and claims](/teams/expenses-and-claims#per-diem-and-mileage).
