Call a business places a real phone call. Mazer introduces itself as an assistant calling for your traveller, asks your questions (in English unless you set `language`), gets through the hotel's phone menu to a person, and hangs up politely. You get each answer, a one-line outcome and the full transcript.

```bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/v1/jobs" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRIPMAZER_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "kind": "call_business", "input": {
        "business": { "name": "Taj Malabar Resort & Spa", "city": "Kochi" },
        "on_behalf_of": "Asha Rao",
        "questions": [ "Can I check in early, around 10 am on 1 December?",
                       "Do you offer an airport pickup, and what does it cost?" ],
        "facts": [ "The booking is under Asha Rao, 1 to 3 December, one room." ],
        "voice": "kore", "max_minutes": 5 } }'
```

| Input | |
| --- | --- |
| `business` | `name` and `city`; optional `phone`, which must be listed on Google Maps or the business's own website, or printed in `booking` |
| `booking` | Optional: the booking confirmation, for a business Google Maps doesn't list. One of `text`, `email` (`subject`, `body`) or `pdf` (`base64`) |
| `on_behalf_of` | The traveller's name, said at the start of the call |
| `questions` | 1 to 8 questions |
| `facts` | Up to 10 things the business may need, like the booking name and dates |
| `language` | Optional; English by default |
| `voice` | Optional; the voice that speaks. `kore` by default ([all voices](#voices)) |
| `max_minutes` | 2 to 10 (default 6) |

A call takes a few minutes, so the job answers `202` and finishes later: wait for the `job.succeeded` webhook or read `GET /v1/jobs/{id}`. The result:

```json
{
  "business": { "name": "Taj Malabar Resort & Spa, Cochin", "phone": "+91…" },
  "number_source": "google_maps",
  "status": "answered",
  "outcome": "Spoke to the front desk; early check-in is possible from 10 am if a room is ready.",
  "answers": [
    { "question": "Can I check in early, around 10 am on 1 December?", "answer": "Yes, from 10 am if a room is ready." },
    { "question": "Do you offer an airport pickup, and what does it cost?", "answer": null }
  ],
  "transcript": [ { "speaker": "agent", "text": "Hello, this is an assistant calling on behalf of Asha Rao…" } ],
  "seconds": 153,
  "charge": { "minutes": 3, "basis": "standard" }
}
```

`status` is `answered`, `automated_menu`, `voicemail`, `no_answer` or `wrong_number`. `number_source` says how the number was proven: `google_maps`, `business_website` or `booking`. Only an answered call is charged by the minute; the rest cost nothing, or the phone line's own cost when the line picked up. A call that couldn't be placed fails the job with nothing charged, and the same business isn't rung twice within 15 minutes.

## The rules Mazer keeps

- **Businesses only.** Mazer calls a number only when it can prove the number belongs to the business you name: Google Maps lists it, the business's own website shows it, or the booking you send prints it. It never calls private people.
- **Honest about what it is.** It opens as an assistant calling on your traveller's behalf, and if anyone asks whether it's an AI, it says yes straight away. It never pretends to be a person.
- **Asks, never commits.** It doesn't book, change, cancel or agree to pay, and never reads out card details. If the business needs a payment, it says the traveller will follow up.
- **Where it calls today:** India, the US and Canada, the UK, Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia.

## Voices

The same voices as the TripMazer app. The accent is used on calls in English; in any other language, the voice speaks without it.

## Businesses Google Maps doesn't list

A homestay, a driver or a small tour operator often isn't on Google Maps, or is listed without a phone. Mazer first looks for the number on the business's own website. If it isn't there, send the booking confirmation with the number it prints:

```json
{
  "business": { "name": "Kayal Breeze Homestay", "city": "Alleppey", "phone": "+91 98950 00000" },
  "booking": { "text": "Booking confirmed 8B44C8. Kayal Breeze Homestay, Alleppey. Host phone +91 98950 00000. Guest Asha Rao, 12–13 December." },
  "on_behalf_of": "Asha Rao",
  "questions": [ "Can we check in at 10 am?" ]
}
```

The booking must name the business and print that exact number; otherwise the job fails with `business_not_in_booking` or `number_not_in_booking`, before anything is dialled. On these calls Mazer opens by naming the booking, so the person knows straight away whether you have the right place.

Numbers from a booking have a few extra limits, so the calling line stays trusted by phone networks:

- Your account has topped up at least once (`needs_top_up` otherwise). Numbers from Google Maps and websites work on the free credit.
- The same number at most 3 times a week (`number_call_limit`), and 10 such calls a day, raised to 50 once your account has a clean record (`daily_call_limit`).

## Wrong numbers

If the person says it's the wrong number, or knows of no such booking, Mazer apologises and hangs up. The job finishes with `status: "wrong_number"`, nothing is charged, and your account won't call that number again. A number reported wrong by two different accounts isn't called by anyone.

<Tip>A test key never dials: you get a sample call with a made-up transcript, so you can build against the result safely.</Tip>
